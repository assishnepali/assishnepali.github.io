About this prototype

What it does, what it does not do, and how it was built.

ProcureFlow — an AI-assisted procurement portfolio prototype by Assish Sapkota. All organisations and transactions are fictional.

The purchasing problem

A buyer receives a need, asks several suppliers for prices, and then has to choose one. The choice is rarely just the lowest unit price: freight, minimum order quantities, quotation validity, payment terms and above all the delivery date change the answer. The difficult part is not the arithmetic, it is being able to show later why a supplier was chosen, and what still needs chasing after the order was placed.

The intended user

A junior buyer or purchasing assistant handling routine repeat purchases: packaging, consumables, components. One person, one product per request, no approval hierarchy.

How the workflow supports the decision

  1. Request — the need is written down once: specification, quantity, unit, required date, location.
  2. Quotations — each quotation belongs to that request, so only identical specifications, quantities and units are ever compared.
  3. Comparison — every quotation is costed the same way and anything that makes it unusable is shown, not hidden.
  4. Decision — the buyer records which quotation was chosen and why. Overriding the lowest-cost option, or accepting a late delivery, requires a written reason.
  5. Purchase order — created from the chosen quotation, with a frozen snapshot of the price, terms and reasoning.
  6. Receipts — deliveries are recorded as they arrive, so the outstanding quantity and the status are always calculated, never typed.

The formulas, and their limits

Total quoted cost = quantity × unit price + freight + other charges − discount
Effective unit cost = total quoted cost ÷ quantity
Outstanding quantity = quantity ordered − sum of recorded receipts
Outstanding goods value = outstanding quantity × purchase order unit price

What these formulas deliberately do not include:

  • No VAT and no tax handling. Every figure is EUR excluding VAT.
  • No currency conversion. This version is EUR only.
  • No cost of capital, so longer payment terms are shown as a fact, not converted into money.
  • No stock-out cost, so "late" is flagged but never priced.
  • No quality history, supplier risk or total cost of ownership.
  • No weighted scoring. A weighted score would hide the judgement inside invented numbers.

This means the recommendation answers one narrow question: which compliant quotation that can arrive in time has the lowest total quoted cost? Everything else is left to the buyer, which is why a written reason is required to depart from it.

Why localStorage is only suitable for a demonstration

  • Data is saved in one browser on one device. It does not follow you to your phone or another browser.
  • Clearing browsing data, or using a private window, removes it.
  • Two people cannot work on the same records, and there is no server backup.
  • Anyone using the browser can edit the stored data directly, so the change history is a local activity history for convenience — not a secure audit trail and not evidence.

Use Backup to JSON before clearing your browser, and Restore to bring the records back.

My contribution, and the role of AI assistance

I defined the purchasing problem, the workflow and the rules this prototype follows: what makes a quotation comparable, why lateness must not be hidden inside a score, which figures belong on the first screen, and what a buyer needs to see after an order is placed. I specified the scenario and the expected results, reviewed the behaviour against them and directed the changes.

The code was written with AI assistance (Claude). I am not claiming I wrote every line unaided, that this has been formally tested, that it produced business savings, that I have SAP experience, or that anyone uses it. It is a learning and demonstration project.

Two-minute demonstration

  1. Overview (15s) — "Everything needing action is at the top: an overdue order with 250 units outstanding, and requests waiting for a decision."
  2. Sourcing, REQ-2026-0001 (45s) — "1,000 identical boxes in ten days. Three quotations, same specification. Baltic Kraft is cheapest at €1,050 but delivers in fifteen days, so it misses the deadline. Vermeer at €1,180 is the cheapest that arrives in time — €70 less than Lahtinen and €130 more than Baltic Kraft."
  3. Decision (20s) — "I record the choice and the reason. If I pick a different supplier or accept a late delivery, the app makes me write why."
  4. Purchase order (20s) — "The order is created from the quotation, keeping a snapshot of the price and reasoning, so editing the supplier later cannot rewrite the decision."
  5. Receipt (20s) — "600 of 1,000 arrive, 400 outstanding, status becomes Partially received automatically. The original promised date is kept, so lateness is not erased."

Five questions I should be able to answer

  1. Why is the cheapest quotation not automatically the right one here, and what would change your answer?
  2. How is total quoted cost calculated, and what does it deliberately leave out?
  3. What makes two quotations comparable, and what do you do with one that is not?
  4. Why keep the original supplier-confirmed date when a new expected date arrives?
  5. Why is a written reason required to override the suggested quotation or accept a late delivery?
